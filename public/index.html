Why I made this

I'm a film producer. We plan shoots months ahead. I live in my calendar app, scheduling crew, locations, equipment. Weather determines everything: can we shoot exteriors? Do we need rain covers? Should we push the shoot day?

I kept switching between calendar and weather apps. Apple Calendar only shows weather if an event has a location. Google Calendar killed their weather feature. The services that exist are broken, limited to 7 days, or want $5/month for something that should be free.

I just wanted to glance at next Tuesday and see if it's going to rain. So every day shows the forecast for 16 days out, then the typical weather for that date, marked ≈, out to a year. It updates every morning.

Questions

How do I add weather to Apple Calendar?

Choose Apple Calendar above and tap your city. Calendar asks whether to subscribe; tap Subscribe. On iPhone the forecast appears on every day in the Calendar app. It updates by itself; on a Mac, right-click the calendar and choose Get Info to set how often.

How do I add weather to Google Calendar?

On a computer, choose Google Calendar above and click your city. Google Calendar opens with the weather calendar ready to add. Once added it syncs to the Google Calendar app on your phone. Google refreshes subscribed calendars on its own schedule, usually about once a day.

How do I add weather to Outlook?

Choose Outlook and click your city to subscribe in Outlook on the web. For a work or school account, choose Something else to copy the link, then in Outlook go to Add calendar, Subscribe from web, and paste it.

What does ≈ mean on some days?

Past the 16-day forecast, each day shows typical weather for that date, the average of the last 10 years. It's marked ≈ so you never mistake it for a forecast. The real forecast replaces it about 16 days out.

What does 💧 mean?

A 40% or higher chance of rain or snow that day, with the percentage. Tap any day in your calendar for wind, UV, sunrise and sunset.

Does it cost anything?

No. No account, no tracking. Weather data comes from Open-Meteo.
